**H-K10 — Hypertokens: the unit the trunk reads is a bound vector, not a text piece — identity ⊛ place ⊛ time ⊛ role ⊛ store address — so a verified fact is one input unit that the trunk reads back by algebra, and a span of language is one unit of a length chosen by information rather than by a merge table.** Source: Nick, 2026-10-20 ("if tokens are the problem why not simply replace them by something more efficient? like hypertokens (tentative name)"; "and for language are we condemned to use tokens?"). H-S12 is the first hypertoken (a token bound with its place), the scene `V = Σ e_i ⊛ S(p_i)` is the first hypertoken store, and the read mid-trunk (`SpatialLink.read`) is how the trunk uses one. Inspiration, named for the difference: byte patches sized by entropy, sentence-level concept units, text diffusion over spans, multi-token prediction — none keeps an exact decode; ours does (the lattice theorem). **Three problems one unit causes, and what the hypertoken does to each.** A subword carries ~1 byte of identity and no structure, so the trunk rebuilds structure from statistics — that is the 100–1,000 sightings of H-K7; with the structure bound in at the input, a fact given *once* is read back exactly (the read is algebra, the gate learns *when*). Every piece is a forward pass, so the cost is paid per piece, not per meaning — a span of n pieces as one unit runs the trunk once (H-K8's energy argument compounds). A fact of ~20 pieces is 20 places to be wrong — a fact as one unit is decoded whole from the store. **Two halves, honestly separated.** *Input (cheap, ours to choose):* a span is `Σ_i e(piece_i) ⊛ S(i)`, a bag with order, exactly decodable by unbinding at `S(i)`, length bounded by the capacity law (M ln M < d/2: ~55 pieces at d = 1024, ~35 at d = 256); the boundary comes from the host (a word, a name, a fact, a sentence — the ask loop's grammar and `route()` are the linker's start) or from an entropy rule; language words stay words — hypertokens replace the fact-bearing spans and add structure to the rest. *Output (the hard half):* predicting the next span is predicting a vector, not a distribution over 128k symbols — a vector cannot say "70% Paris, 30% Lyon"; ambiguity becomes a superposition, and a superposition decoded blindly is a blend. Routes: keep piece-level output for free text and use hypertokens at the input only (this entry's gate), or emit a point in the Hilbert space that the *store* decodes — nearest entity, unbind for relation and object — exact for the structured part of what the model says and free of the vocabulary softmax (Group C's bottleneck), silent on the rest. **What it brings:** a fact seen once and answered; the trunk run once per meaning; the fact half of the corpus at one unit per fact (H-K7's factor before any sightings argument). **Advantage over conventional models:** their token is a text piece and structure is a hope; ours is a bound vector and structure is a theorem. **Mechanism, to build (flag-gated, < 300 lines):** `validation/exp_k10_hypertoken.py` — a tiny `train_base` trunk with `SpatialLink` (the existing channel, unchanged): documents of 12 facts `s r o` with the object's token placed at the fact's address `cell(s) + offset(r)` (an entity's cell a hash into a 1,024³ lattice, a relation's offset a lattice step the kernel separates), ~150 tokens of filler language, then questions `Q s r HERE → o` with `HERE` placed at the same address — the questions fall beyond the attention window (64), the facts are random per document (never seen in training), so the answer can come only from the scene's algebraic read; the control is the same documents with nothing placed (text; the plain path, bit-identical). **Gates (pre-registered):** (1) one sighting — held-out documents' questions answered ≥ 90% through the channel where the text control sits at chance beyond the window (and at ≥ 80% *inside* the window, to show attention can do it only within reach); (2) the text goldens bit-identical with the channel off (SPEC) and the filler language's held-out loss within 0.02 of the control's; (3) spans — a word of up to 8 pieces presented as one bound unit at the input, the trunk's next-piece loss on the following text within 0.05 of the piece-by-piece baseline at ≤ 1/4 the forward passes; (4) output — on the wiki world, the trunk's emitted vector decoded by the store (nearest entity) answers ≥ the text head's exact-match on the same questions. **Kill:** gate 1 below 60% (the trunk cannot learn to query the scene for a fact it was shown once; then hypertokens stay a host-side object), or gate 2's loss worse by 0.05.
