[0038][0037] Nick: the everyday table and the tense check on by default; three things that surfaced, fixed

Nick, 2026-10-24, in Claude Code's chat:
- CB_EVERYDAY is on by default; CB_EVERYDAY=0 turns it off.
- The tense check is on always, books or not. tense=None now means on; the server's --no-tense turns it off
  (--tense is kept, and does nothing).
- The time rule follows the tense check, as Cowork measured.

Three things surfaced when they came on:
1. Nested questions. The table read "What is the capital of the country of citizenship of Marie?" as one hop,
   with "country of citizenship of Marie" as the name. EverydayForms now leaves a seed that starts with a
   relation it names to the model's plan ("Bank of America" and "national anthem of France" are still names).
2. The host's own grammar for an event. "What is the date of birth of X?" was refused, because the tense check
   read its "is" as asking about the birth in the present. question_forms.noun_tense: an event named as a noun
   asks no tense.
   - "Where is X born?" is still refused.
   - A state keeps its auxiliary: "What is the currency of Germany?" still refuses the Deutsche Mark.
3. 0037's answers and the checks.
   - The checks after a walk (the bulk veto, tense, "as of", the time rule, the one-witness hedge) moved
     verbatim from AskLoop._ask into AskMemory._checked. They run only when the walk and the record both say
     verified, so a namesake's refusal is never given an "As of 2019, None." reply.
   - Each "several fit" walk passes the same checks, so an ended value is not said for one namesake.
   - The namesakes' "there is also ..." line comes after the "as of", instead of being overwritten.
   - With the flag off, the loop is unchanged: after_walk stays where it was.

Tests that pinned the old defaults now name the switch they mean: tense=False / time_rule=False in
test_hdc_store and test_time_rule, everyday=False for the emitter's alias path in test_ask. New pins: the
nested guard, on by default and off with CB_EVERYDAY=0, noun_tense, and each namesake's walk checked.
