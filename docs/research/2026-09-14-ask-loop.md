# The loop, live: natural questions in the control panel — 13–14 September

The verified-program loop had only ever run over a dataset. Tonight it answers one question at a
time, typed into the control panel, with every step on the screen as it happens — and the first
evening of that found one wrong answer spoken, three refusals that were the source's, and one
minute of hashing per question. Each is a rule now, each pinned. Kill line: **0 wrong answers spoken.**

## What was built

`standin/ask.py` — `AskLoop.ask(question)`: the emitter proposes a plan, the host disposes
(`covers`, levers 4–7), the VM verifies, the Wikidata source fills the store through the gate with
provenance — exactly exp_r11's per-row path, as a service. `standin/serve_api.py --ask` mounts it
behind `POST /ask`; every event of the run (`cubbyllm/reasoning/events.py`) streams on
`/loop/stream`, and the panel (`dashboard/control_panel.html`, served at `/panel`) has an ask box:
the question's tree opens the moment its root event arrives and redraws per hop, the answer or the
refusal lands in the status line. `python standin/serve_api.py --gguf <emitter> --ask`, then `/panel`.

**Links between questions** (Nick, 13 Sep: "there needs to be linked between answers"). The store
is one, so what one question learned is what the next one walks on. The panel now indexes every
fact walked or admitted and every entity fetched by the question that touched it: a shared node is
ringed (⇄) in the tree, the inspector lists the other questions that carry the same fact or entity
(click to jump to it), and the galaxy draws a line between every pair of linked questions.

## The three refusals that were the source's — entity resolution

Nick's case: *What is the first name of Bill Haslam's father?* refused; *What is the given name of
the father of Bill Haslam?* walks hop 0 (`Jim Haslam is the father of Bill Haslam`) and stalls.
Both stalled on the same thing: asked about **Jim Haslam**, the source returned nothing, because
"Jim Haslam" is the label of one item and an alias of another (Jimmy Haslam, his son), and the rule
since the ceiling probe was *a label shared by two items is a refusal, never a pick*. **Marie Curie**
was refused outright: a physicist, a 2022 book edition, a Montreal metro station and a ferry share
the label. That rule, kept as it was, would refuse most famous people.

`WikidataSource.resolve` now has three tiers, each deterministic, none a pick by rank:

1. **Linked** — the object of a claim this source already served *is* an item: when Bill Haslam's
   facts came in, `Jim Haslam` was recorded as Q6195484, the item the `father` claim pointed at.
   The walk's next hop asks about that item, not about a string. A name that named two different
   items in served claims is not linked (the tier stands aside). Persisted beside the cache.
2. **One exact hit** (label or alias equal to the query), as before.
3. **The question decides** among several exact hits: first the items whose claims carry the
   property the walk needs next (`learn.wanted_relations`: the tail's relation for the seed, the
   next plan relation for the object the walk stopped at — the book and the ferry have no date of
   birth); then, among those, the item whose *label* is the name the question used over items that
   only carry it as an alias (Jim over Jimmy). Still more than one — the James Youngs — is still
   refused. A question-decided pick is not remembered as the name's item.

Both Haslam wordings now verify **James**; Marie Curie's birth date and birthplace verify. The
fetch event carries `how` (`linked` / `exact` / `relation (the question's next hop)` / `+ label
over alias`), `item` and the ambiguity list, so the panel shows which tier resolved what.
Residue, on record: a question that is itself ambiguous ("What is the country of Obama?" — a town
in Japan carries that exact label) resolves to the item literally named.

## The wrong answer, and its two rules

*Who is the mother of Justin Trudeau?* → **Pierre Trudeau, verified.** The trace: the plan said
`mother`; the wiki world holds `father` and not `mother`; the property table named `mother` (it is
P25) but the store lacked it; the synonym oracle behind the table (lever 5, WordNet) offered
`father`, `sire`, `beget` — the **verb** sense of "mother" — and lever 4, pooling every resolver's
labels and keeping the held ones, rewrote the plan to `father` and the VM verified the father.

* **The first resolver that names a wording decides.** A relation the table knows and the store
  lacks is a fact to *fetch*, not a word to paraphrase; the oracle speaks only for wordings the table
  has no form of (`dad`). Pinned: the same store, the same oracle, and the answer is Margaret Trudeau
  after one fetch.
* **The lexicon words a relation with noun senses only.** A relation is a noun phrase; a verb sense
  of its word is another word. `relations('mother')` no longer contains `father`. Pinned.

## A minute of hashing

The gate's provenance snapshot hashed every stored text, sorted, per gated fact: on the wiki world
(552k texts) 0.35 s a fact, a minute for Marie Curie's 172. Now a running set hash kept on the store
(a sum of per-fact digests mod 2^128 — order-independent, equal to the fresh computation, extended
only over what was appended). Pinned; the gate is instant again.

## LFM2.5-2.6B as a fact source — measured (`exp_r11_search_learn_wikidata_lfm`)

Nick's ask (13 Sep): the emitter's base model "is probably already having a bunch of facts". The
source (`standin/lfm_source.py`) asks it, few-shot, for an entity's facts in the store's own form,
parses them into Triples, and the gate admits them **latent**: stored with provenance `lfm (latent)`,
never spoken until a second source attests. Same 600 SimpleQA, seed 7, the same emitter and lexicon:
180 questions fetched, **450 facts admitted (latent)**, 0 verified — and **7 answers held by the latent
tier, all 7 wrong** (`latent:WRONG 7`). Cross-checked against Wikidata's cached facts where the
entity resolves: **agree 1, disagree 28** (dates of birth 0 agree / 22 disagree), 102 unheld
relations, 319 entities the cache does not know. On this distribution the base model's parametric
facts are nearly always wrong, and the latent tier is what made that safe to find out: nothing it
said was spoken. The source stays for what it may be good at (attestation on common entities); it
is not a source of SimpleQA answers.

## The battery

exp_r11 SimpleQA 600 (`_lev9`), the resolver order, the noun lexicon and the resolution tiers in:
**6 verified, 5 correct, 1 near, 0 wrong** (`_lev8`: 7 / 5 / 2 / 0). The near that left is Kafr
al-Awamid's *district*: the table names `district` with two held properties and the plan is now
refused as `ambiguous_relation` ahead of the walk, where the pooled resolvers had let a one-label
reading through and spoken *Az-Zabdani Subdistrict* — a near, never a correct. The five correct
answers are the same five. The resolution tiers show in the fetch counts: 177 questions fetched,
**2,406 facts admitted** against 1,328 (entities that used to be refused as shared names now
resolve), 81 live calls against 21. The kill line holds; the trade was one near for one honest refusal.
