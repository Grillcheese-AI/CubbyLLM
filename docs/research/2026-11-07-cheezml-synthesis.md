# CheezML v0.1: what the panel produced, what was checked, what was kept (2026-11-07)

Nick asked the OpenRouter panel for one record vocabulary covering everything we have ("give it to our ai openrouter
panel so they can generate one with everything we have instead?"). Eight models answered the same brief
(`2026-11-07-cheezml-brief.md`); their full answers are in `2026-11-07-cheezml-panel.md` (8 of 8, $2.41). Every answer
was then checked by tools, not by reading. The merged draft is in `2026-11-07-cheezml/`. It is a draft: order 0064
turns it into the vocabulary the host writes.

**What it is, in plain words:** one way to write down everything Cubby does (an answer and its receipt, a refusal and
its reason, a MoWM routing choice, a reviewed invoice field) so that one checker can read any of it. A record only
says what actually ran.

## 1. The tool check of each answer

Each answer's JSON-LD was expanded to RDF with its own `@context`, its Turtle parsed, its records validated with
pySHACL against its own shapes, and its CubeLang programs run through `cubelang check` (main checkout's release
binary, read only).

| Model | JSON-LD blocks parse | Triples | Terms that don't expand to IRIs | Turtle parses | Shapes | Own records conform | Programs compile |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|:---:|---:|
| GPT-6.1 sol pro | 7 of 7 | 191 | 0 | 1 of 1 | 6 | yes | 0 of 2 |
| Gemini 4 argon | 7 of 7 | 201 | 0 | 1 of 1 | 4 | yes | 0 of 2 |
| Kimi K3.1 pro | 7 of 7 | 219 | 0 | 1 of 1 | 3 | no | 0 of 2 |
| Grok 4.7 | 8 of 8 | 350 | 6 | 1 of 1 | 5 | no | 0 of 2 |
| GLM 5.3 | 7 of 7 | 103 | 4 | 1 of 1 | 3 | no | 0 of 2 |
| Qwen 3.8 max | 7 of 7 | 105 | 5 | 1 of 3 | 1 | no | 0 of 2 |
| DeepSeek V4 pro | 7 of 7 | 96 | 6 | 0 of 1 | 0 | none to run | 0 of 2 |
| Claude Opus 5.5 (cut off at its token limit) | 7 of 7 | 365 | 6 | none | 0 | none to run | none |

- **Only GPT-6.1 and Gemini passed every check** they could be given. The synthesis starts from those two answers
  and takes the other answers' choices where most of the panel agreed and the brief allows it.
- **Not one of the 14 programs compiles.** They fail at their first lines: `//` comments, storage fields without
  `name: type`, invented attributes and conditions (`if x clears y`). Four answers claimed their programs fit the
  compiler. So the vocabulary has no programs in it; they get written against the compiler (order 0064, step 5).
- **The Decision Ontology guess was wrong six different ways.** The published `do.ttl` declares its terms under
  `http://example.com/ns/dec#`, a placeholder namespace, and has no `is_satisfied_by` or `is_violated_by` (its README
  lists them). CheezML mints its own decision terms and links them to the published ones with `skos:closeMatch`.

## 2. What the draft decides (the full list, with the panel's votes, is in `disagreements.md`)

- **The answer or the refusal is the Decision.** The certificate is the receipt: written by the host, generated by one
  VM run that returned, reading fact versions with their source and revision. Each check run points at the option it
  was satisfied or violated by.
- **Closed shapes for certificates and refusals.** A truth score, a rationale or a status field makes the record
  fail. Readable text exists only as template output from codes.
- **Two clocks by reuse:** valid time is an OWL-Time interval; recorded time is PROV's generation time, and a
  correction closes the old version with `prov:invalidatedAtTime`.
- **Thresholds carry their calibration** (screen, data by hash, date, encoder, deployment). A bare number fails.
- **The routing record is checked against its own numbers.** SPARQL rules check that margin = best − runner-up and
  that "route" or "spawn" follows from the values and thresholds. Nothing is stored as "passed".
- **Splits** must go to depth parent + 1, below z_max, with the residual by hash. Router spawns and z+1 splits are
  separate record types, because they follow different rules and counters.
- **A rejected helper is retired, never deleted.** Its reason is the code of the gate that failed, and the case that
  broke it is kept.
- **A committed invoice field** needs an approving review; its source is the reviewer; the value must appear in the
  region's text as read.
- **Hashes, not blobs.** No vectors, weights, programs or bytecode inside a record.

## 3. Validation of the draft

Run with `tools/validate.py` (rdflib 7.6, pySHACL 0.40, pyld as a second JSON-LD processor). Output in
`validation.txt`.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Shapes parse | 1,159 triples; 33 node shapes; 148 property constraints; 17 SPARQL rules; every constraint has a message |
| The six example records conform | 6 of 6 |
| The broken records fail, each with its expected message | 10 of 10 |
| IRIs outside the allowed namespaces, placeholders inside IRIs, ill-typed literals | 0, 0, 0 |
| Second JSON-LD processor (pyld) agrees on triple counts | yes |
| CheezML terms used but not defined in `vocabulary.md` | 0 |

The ten broken records cover: a certificate by agreement, a certificate with a truth score, a refusal with no
reason, a conflict with no kind, a threshold with no calibration, a route that contradicts its margin, a split beyond
z_max, a rejected helper with no breaking case, a committed field with no source, and a value missing from its
region's text.

**What this proves and what it doesn't:** the examples are consistent with the shapes, and the shapes catch the
faults they were built to catch. Every number in the examples is illustrative. Nothing here was written by the host
from a real run yet; that is order 0064's acceptance.

## 4. Not settled

The open questions are in `disagreements.md` §3 and §5. The ones only the code can answer (Claude Code reads them off
the repositories in 0064 step 1): whether "clears" is > or ≥; what a world's tau is compared with, and whether it is
`tau_spawn`; which counter issues a bridge-spawned world's id; whether router pruning deletes; the hash algorithm; how
the store holds statement content. The one that is Nick's: the second-approval amount.
