# 2026-09-12 — the striatum: proposer arbitration by reward prediction error

**The ask** (Nick, "what about dopamine as reward?"). Dopamine is not the reward; it is the reward
prediction error — what happened minus what was expected — and that error is what moves the striatum's
choices. CubbyLLM has a reward nobody can fake: the gate's verdict. So: an expected value per (proposer,
question shape); after every try, delta = reward − expected, expectation += α·delta; the proposers are asked
best-first. Rewards: certified +1; refused 0, **never negative** (a "don't know" that costs anything teaches
the loop to guess); wrong −5, far below −1, so the expected value of guessing stays below refusing — and only
from an audit against gold or a user's explicit word, never a model's opinion of itself. The tonic level
(recent mean reward) gates exploration. Every delta is on the record. `cubbyllm/reasoning/striatum.py`, 7 pins.

**Measured** (`exp_r15`): every proposer tried on every question once — 200 matched chains × 4 forms
(grammar 189 certified, hippocampus 395, emitter 320, **0 wrong each**) plus the SimpleQA 600 from the
record (emitter, hippocampus, frontier rows) — then three policies replayed on the same 1,400-question
stream. Every policy certifies the same 589 (each tries until one certifies); the measure is cost.

| policy | proposals | emitter calls | seconds | wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed order (grammar → hippocampus → emitter → frontier) | 3,037 | 838 | 1,313 | 0 |
| striatum, reward only | 2,786 | 1,080 | 1,446 | 0 |
| striatum, effort-aware (cost weight 1.0) | 2,862 | 839 | 1,314 | 0 |
| oracle (cheapest certifying proposer first, hindsight) | 2,547 | 838 | 1,306 | 0 |

Two honest readings. (1) **The fixed order already sits on the oracle floor** — 1,313 s against 1,306 —
because it was built cheap-first: the free grammar, then the 50 ms hippocampus, then the 1 s emitter. There
is nothing for arbitration to win here in time, and the effort-aware striatum matches it (1,314 s). (2) The
reward-only striatum is measurably worse: it moved the emitter ahead of the grammar wherever the emitter
certified a little more often, 8% fewer proposals and 242 more emitter calls. Effort is part of the circuit
— in the brain too — and `order()` now scores expected reward minus cost. Replays run in seconds on the
recorded stream (`exp_r15_replay.py`), so the policy questions cost no GPU.

**What the striatum is for, then.** Not this stream. It is for the streams where the cheap-first order is
wrong: a hippocampus with half a million episodes whose recall stops being free; a frontier proposer that
costs money and certifies more; a shape the emitter never gets and the hippocampus always does, or the
reverse. And its table is an honest self-model the system did not have: after 1,400 questions it expects
the grammar to certify 44% of "what is the R of the R of E" and nothing to certify most SimpleQA shapes —
the exact numbers the record shows, learned from outcomes alone. The two remaining uses from the design —
surprise-weighted episode utility, and a gen-4 pass where the gate's verdict moves the emitter's weights
(RL with a verifiable reward, which the no-self-play invariant permits because the verifier is not the
model) — are built on this signal.
